Paths through timber, not a bare lot

Not every job is a full clear. Plenty of landowners around Morgantown, Fairmont, Bridgeport, and Clarksburg just need a way through the woods: a walking trail, a path for an ATV, a driveway cut to a future homesite, or a fence line that has disappeared into brush. Forestry mulching can open a corridor and leave the timber on either side standing.

Hunting land is a common reason for this work. A food plot, a path to a stand, or a cleaner edge around a field can be mulched without opening the entire property. If that is the goal, say how wide you want the path and whether the cuttings should stay as a chip layer underfoot.

Fence lines, driveways, and tight access

Fence line clearing is often a narrow, grown-in strip along a pasture or a property line. Driveway paths can be steeper, especially in the hills above Morgantown or on a hollow outside Clarksburg. Access matters as much as width. Tell the crew how a machine would get to the start of the path, and whether wires, a creek, or a neighbor's line sits close to the work.

If you later want the rest of the underbrush opened, that can be planned as a second step. See brush clearing and lot clearing if the trail is only the first part of a larger plan.
