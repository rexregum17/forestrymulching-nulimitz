Open the woods without taking every tree

Selective clearing is for landowners who want the property opened, not stripped. The mulcher takes underbrush, saplings, and marked trees, and leaves the mature timber, a tree line, or a shaded yard in place. That is a common request on steep lots around Morgantown, on older family land near Fairmont, and on homesites in Bridgeport and Clarksburg where a view or a windbreak matters.

The work still starts with a walk of the property, or with photos and a clear list of what should stay. Ribbon or a simple map helps. If you want more light in the understory, a cleaner look from the house, or room to mow between trees, say that in the form.

A planned finish, not a bare hillside

Because the chips stay on the ground, selective work often leaves a walkable, finished look under the trees. It is not a timber harvest, and it is not a grading job. Large trees that have to come down may still need to be cut first. If a homesite or a driveway is part of the same plan, see lot clearing and describe both needs in one request.

Selective clearing also pairs well with invasive species work when autumn olive or multiflora rose is mixed into woods you otherwise want to keep.

What this work is often used for

Selective clearing is a common choice when a homesite, a yard, or hunting land should be opened without losing the trees you want to keep. It also helps with visibility around a house and with listing prep on a wooded lot that should still feel like woods.
